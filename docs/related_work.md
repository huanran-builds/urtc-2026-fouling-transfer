# Related Work

## Li et al. (2026)

Jiayin Li, Xinpei Lu, Pranav Arun, Jing Xu, Fausto Gallucci, Sirui Li, and Annemie Bogaerts. “Machine learning to predict plasma-based CO2 conversion in dielectric barrier discharge reactors.” *Green Chemistry* **28**(23), 9804–9821 (2026). DOI: **10.1039/d6gc01077f**.

Li et al. assembled a literature-derived chemistry dataset and showed that conventional random validation substantially overestimated performance relative to source-publication-grouped validation (R² above 0.91 versus 0.441), demonstrating that publication-level structure can produce misleadingly optimistic results. They did not attempt retrospective batch correction or domain adaptation; instead, their recommendation was prospective—more standardized data collection and reporting going forward—leaving open how to recover generalization from a heterogeneous literature dataset that already exists.

## Xu et al. (2026)

WeiHang Xu, LiHua Bai, Ji Qi, XiaoYing Sun, BaiRan Wang, Zhen Zhao, and Bo Li. “A natural language processing to causality framework for robust knowledge extraction of CO2 hydrogenation with batch effect control.” *npj Computational Materials* **12**, 191 (2026). DOI: **10.1038/s41524-026-02065-2**.

Xu et al. constructed a literature-extracted catalysis dataset and explicitly treated source DOI as a batch, using DOI-level batch-effect control and benchmarking methods including ComBat and RUV-PC to reduce between-study variation. Their predictive stage still used an 80/20 random split, while DOI grouping and batch control were applied primarily in the causal-analysis framework, so they did not test whether adaptation could improve prediction on a completely unseen source publication.

## Austin et al. (2025) — DEBIAS-M

George I. Austin, Aya Brown Kav, Shahd ElNaggar, Heekuk Park, Jana Biermann, Anne-Catrin Uhlemann, Itsik Pe’er, and Tal Korem. “Processing-bias correction with DEBIAS-M improves cross-study generalization of microbiome-based prediction models.” *Nature Microbiology* **10**(4), 897–911 (2025). DOI: **10.1038/s41564-025-01954-4**.

Austin et al. introduced DEBIAS-M to improve cross-study generalization in microbiome prediction and evaluated an online setting in which a fitted model adapts to samples from a previously unobserved study without using the held-out outcome labels. This closely matches the unlabeled target-study adaptation setting of our work, but their inputs are pooled microbiome datasets generated by separate studies rather than a literature-extracted table assembled from experimental results reported across publications.

## Sun, Feng, and Saenko (2016) — CORAL

Baochen Sun, Jiashi Feng, and Kate Saenko. “Return of Frustratingly Easy Domain Adaptation.” *Proceedings of the Thirtieth AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence* **30**(1), 2058–2065 (2016). DOI: **10.1609/aaai.v30i1.10306**.

Sun, Feng, and Saenko introduced CORrelation ALignment (CORAL), a general-purpose unsupervised domain-adaptation method that aligns the second-order statistics of source and unlabeled target features. CORAL was developed for computer-vision domain shifts rather than literature-curated scientific data, so it is a methodological tool for our adaptation step rather than a competing study-level literature result.

## Closest near-miss

Jianmin Guo, Zhongling Wang, Shouyao Yang, Yufeng Xu, Ting Huang, Yong Yang, and Huiyong Hu. “Multi-task deep learning with transfer learning for UHPC compressive and flexural strength prediction and inverse mix design.” *Case Studies in Construction Materials* **24**, e06088 (2026). DOI: **10.1016/j.cscm.2026.e06088**.

Guo et al. used CORAL to transfer from a literature-derived UHPC source dataset to a small, newly generated local laboratory dataset. This is close methodologically, but the target domain is new laboratory data rather than an unseen publication inside the same literature-extracted table, so it does not test the question addressed here.

## Dataset-source citation verification: Bouasla et al. (2024)

Nabila Bouasla, Sihem Abderrahmane, Zahra Obeizi, Messast Sarah, and Adel Saoudi. “Antimicrobial Activity of ZnS and ZnO-TOP Nanoparticles Againts Pathogenic Bacteria.” *Chemistry & Biodiversity* **21**(12), e202400724 (2024). DOI: **10.1002/cbdv.202400724**.

The DOI landing page verifies the title, complete five-author list, journal, volume 21, issue 12, and article number e202400724. The released Chen MIC table assigns 18 consecutive observations to this DOI: nine organism entries for ZnS and the same nine for ZnO-TOP, supporting the 18-row forward-fill interpretation. The publisher-accessible metadata confirms MIC was one of the antimicrobial assays; direct visual inspection of Tables 3 and 4 should still be retained as the source-level check before the team treats that table-number statement as independently verified.

## Synthesis

Taken together, the problem of publication-level generalization is documented by Li et al., batch correction on literature-extracted data is established by Xu et al., and unlabeled target-study adaptation is demonstrated by Austin et al.; CORAL supplies a general-purpose method for attempting that adaptation. What remains untested is whether unlabeled adaptation to an unseen source publication can recover predictive accuracy on a literature-extracted dataset, which is the question we address.
