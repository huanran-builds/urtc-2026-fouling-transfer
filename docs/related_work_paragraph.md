Li et al. (2026) showed that random validation on literature-curated chemistry data can substantially inflate performance relative to publication-grouped validation (R² > 0.91 versus 0.441); their remedy was prospective, emphasizing more standardized future data collection. Xu et al. (2026) established batch-effect control in literature-extracted catalysis data using source DOI as batch, but their predictive stage retained an ordinary 80/20 random split and did not test adaptation to an unseen publication. Austin et al. (2025) demonstrated leave-one-study-out adaptation with DEBIAS-M using a held-out study’s features without its labels, but on pooled primary microbiome datasets rather than rows extracted from publications. Thus, the remaining question is whether unlabeled correction can recover predictive accuracy on an unseen source publication within a single literature-curated feature table. The closest near-miss, Guo et al. (2026), used CORAL from literature-compiled to newly generated laboratory mixtures, not to an unseen publication within the same table.
